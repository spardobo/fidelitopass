# Original Part A and output template (adapted reference)

These are optional planning patterns, not product requirements. Verify every claim, offer, CTA and available feature against current project docs. Never manufacture proof or risk reversal.

## A1. Intake

Gather in one batch only what is missing: one primary action (trial/demo/buy/waitlist/download), exact offer and conversion event; ICP, problem, three objections, traffic source and visitor awareness; verified logos, testimonials, numbers, case studies, screenshots/videos/GIFs, real guarantees/refunds/cancellation terms; brand voice, design direction and mobile priority. If unknown, state an assumption, but do not assume product facts or publish unsupported claims.

## A2. Page structure

Original suggested above fold: outcome-plus-audience headline, specific subheadline, verb-plus-result CTA, proof signal, product visual. Mid-page: problem/solution, 3–5 benefit outcomes, three-step how-it-works, social proof. Bottom: 6–12 FAQs, risk reversal, repeated CTA. Original also required a B11 tagline reveal. **Use only sections supported by real assets and facts**; no mandatory proof, trial, testimonial, or animated tagline. An honest conceptual visual must be labelled.

## A3. Layout selection

| Type | Use when |
| --- | --- |
| A. Classic hero plus sections | Product understandable from a hero screenshot. |
| B. Long-form story | Education and skepticism matter. |
| C. Minimal conversion page | High-intent traffic or short offer. |
| D. Comparison page | Search intent includes alternatives; often SEO. |

Choose deliberately and explain why, if redesigning structure is in scope.

## A4. Conversion rules

Match ad promise and visual tone to the source without repeating unsupported promises. One primary CTA above fold. Lead with outcomes, not feature inventory; specificity requires verified figures (the original illustrative “4 hours to 15 minutes” is **not** a publishable claim). Handle objections near relevant claims; put actual proof adjacent to claims. Original suggested free trial, free plan, no credit card, cancel anytime or guarantee: **none may be asserted without confirmed terms**. Move FAQs earlier for high friction offers.

## A5. Copywriting

Original headline patterns: “{Outcome} without {pain}”, “The {category} for {audience}”, “Ship {result} in {time}”. Subheadline: one or two sentences clarifying product and audience. CTA: verb plus actual destination/result, not generic “Learn more”/“Submit”; original sample “Start free trial” must not imply a free offer. Benefit bullets: bold benefit plus factual supporting detail (original “generate three layout variants in one click” was illustrative only). B1's hyphen/orphan advice is optional and subordinate to natural localized copy.

## A6. Build order

Original section-by-section order: hero, benefits, how it works, proof, FAQ, final CTA. Avoid rebuilding the entire page on every iteration. Omit unsupported sections.

## A7. SEO and AEO

For ad-only/time-bound pages consider noindex; index evergreen pages where search intent matches an accurate promise. If indexed, use clear title, meta description, relevant internal links, plain-language FAQ, and FAQ schema only when appropriate to actual content and search guidance.

## A8. Pitfalls

Competing CTAs, vague “streamline/optimize” value proposition, feature lists without outcomes, buried *real* proof, unreadable mobile layout, unclear next step.

## Original output template and quick strategy checklist

Before code when creating a page from scratch, original suggested: (1) page outline; (2) hero headline/subheadline/CTA/proof line; (3) 3–5 benefits; (4) three steps; (5) 6–12 FAQs; (6) index/noindex and indexed metadata; (7) A/B/C/D layout rationale; then build by section. Omit unverified proof, unsupported features or irrelevant sections rather than filling slots. Check one offer/audience/action, no competing hero CTA, factual specificity, real proof beside claim, verified risk terms only, and deliberate layout.
